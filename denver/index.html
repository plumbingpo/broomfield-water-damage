What Usually Goes Wrong in Denver Homes

Starting at the top of a home and working toward the ground is a handy way to think about water damage. Our crews in Denver follow that same path on an inspection, and these five stops cause the most trouble.

Attics: The top of the house takes on water when shingles are damaged or the seal around a vent pipe gives out. Because insulation absorbs so much, a leak overhead may run through several storms before a mark appears below. Wet insulation stops insulating and keeps the wood beneath it damp. Our part is removing what is saturated and drying the wood, while a roofing contractor closes the opening.

Bathrooms: A tub left filling, a sink with a blocked overflow hole or a toilet that runs over can soak a bathroom floor quickly. Even a tiled floor lets water pass at the grout, the tub edge and the base of the walls. When the bathroom is on an upper story, the water shows up as a wet patch overhead downstairs. If the toilet bowl was the source, assume the water is unsanitary and have the area disinfected.

Walls: Supply lines and drain pipes run inside the walls, and a slow leak there can go on for a long time. You might see trim pulling away, a discolored spot spreading or drywall that gives when pressed. Our Denver technicians scan the area with infrared and probe it with a meter to mark the wet zone. After the pipe is repaired by a plumber, we open just the damp part and dry the cavity.

Tanks: The hot water tank sits out of sight in a garage, closet or utility space and stays full all the time. Worn parts announce themselves with moisture at the top connections, the drain spout or the pressure relief pipe. If the tank lets go, shut the cold inlet valve over the heater and turn off its power or gas supply. We pump out the spill and test the surrounding floor and wall bases, since water tends to run under them.

Drains: The lowest drain in the house is where a sewer blockage shows itself. If murky water comes up in a downstairs shower or floor drain, waste has nowhere to go but back inside. It falls under Category 3, meaning heavily contaminated, so cleanup calls for proper protective gear. Hold off on flushing or running taps, and keep the household clear while our team heads to Denver.

What to Do Before Our Denver Crew Arrives

The first hour is when good intentions cause the most trouble. Here are the slip-ups we run into most on Denver jobs, with the better option for each.

Do not use a household vacuum on water. You risk a shock and a dead vacuum, and a mop with old towels does the job safely. Do not turn on ceiling fans or lights under a wet ceiling, and stay out of rooms where the ceiling is sagging. Leave fitted carpet in place, because pulling it up the wrong way can stretch or rip a carpet we might have saved. Avoid cranking up the heating, as warmth plus moisture encourages mold.

The helpful things are simple: close the valve, prop open cupboards and wardrobes, and stand wet cushions and textiles up to drain. Photograph each room before you shift a single item. Do keep children and pets away from the area. And do make the call early, so Broomfield Restoration can have a crew on the way to Denver while you do the rest.

Simple Ways to Keep a Denver Home Dry

Most of the water damage we see in Denver homes started with something small that gave warning first. Looking over a short list twice a year is enough to find most of those faults.

Start with supply lines. The rubber hoses on washing machines, dishwashers, toilets and ice makers wear out, and braided stainless replacements are inexpensive. Look under every sink for stains, swollen cabinet floors or a musty smell. At the water heater, watch for corrosion or moisture around the bottom, and flush some sediment out annually. Clean gutters and downspouts that discharge well away from the house keep rain out of the foundation.

Find the main water shutoff now, and show every adult in the home how to close it. Small battery leak alarms near the washing machine, water heater and sinks give you an early warning for very little money. A stain that spreads or a floor that feels soft is worth a call, and Denver homeowners are welcome to ask us about it.
